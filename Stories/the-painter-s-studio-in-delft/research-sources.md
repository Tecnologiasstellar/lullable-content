# Research sources — The Painter's Studio in Delft

- https://www.essentialvermeer.com/delft/delft_today/mechelen.html — Vermeer's father bought the Mechelen inn on the Markt in 1641; Johannes grew up there; the father also dealt in pictures.
- https://www.essentialvermeer.com/saint_luke's_guild_delft.html — Vermeer registered as a master in the Delft Guild of Saint Luke in 1653, the year of his marriage to Catharina Bolnes.
- https://www.essentialvermeer.com/delft/delft_today/oude_langendijck.html — the house of Maria Thins on the Oude Langendijk, where Vermeer lived with his family and kept his studio.
- https://www.essentialvermeer.com/technique/vermeer's-studio.html — the upstairs studio: roughly 6.6 m deep, 4 m wide, 3 m high, whitewashed brick walls, windows facing north; the posthumous inventory lists a stone table for grinding paints.
- https://www.essentialvermeer.com/vermeers_rooms.html — black-and-white marble tiles appear in ten or eleven interiors and were probably an invention (Steadman); the map of Holland recurs; the 1676 inventory props (lion-head chair, carpet, blue cloth, black frames).
- https://www.essentialvermeer.com/vermeer's_methodolgy.html — 34 to 37 surviving paintings; Montias's estimate of two or three works a year; Rembrandt, Hals and van Goyen (over a thousand) by contrast.
- https://www.essentialvermeer.com/catalogue/lady_writing.html — the yellow satin jacket with white fur trim in the 1676 inventory; it appears in A Lady Writing, Woman with a Pearl Necklace, Woman with a Lute and Mistress and Maid; the fur possibly rabbit dyed to look like ermine.
- https://www.essentialvermeer.com/palette/palette_grinding_and_materials.html — muller and stone slab, linseed oil binder, circular grinding until no grittiness, storage in bladders or under water, no evidence of an apprentice, pigments from apothecaries.
- https://eu.winsornewton.com/en-row/blogs/articles/ultramarine — lapis lazuli from Afghanistan; ground, mixed with wax, resin or oil, kneaded like dough and washed in lye so the blue sinks out; repeated for finer grades; more precious than gold by weight.
- https://www.essentialvermeer.com/palette/palette_ultramarine.html — Vermeer's ultramarine in the shadows of white cloth, in the shadows of yellow garments, in the gray of walls, mixed with red lake and green earth, and in thin glazes; pure lapis in the Milkmaid's blue.
- https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/out-of-the-blue-vermeers-use-of-ultramarine-in-girl-with-a-pearl-/ — Girl in the Spotlight (2018): ultramarine in the headscarf and mixed into the shadows of the yellow jacket; the background a green glaze of indigo and weld, now darkened.
- https://colourlex.com/project/lead-tin-yellow/ — lead-tin yellow made by heating lead and tin oxides at about 650–800 °C; used from the 14th to the early 18th century, then forgotten and rediscovered in the 20th century.
- https://www.essentialvermeer.com/palette/palette.html — Vermeer's seven basic pigments (white lead, yellow ochre, vermilion, madder, green earth, raw umber, ivory black), up to seventeen in all; madder lake on the Girl's lips; lead-tin yellow and ochre in yellow garments.
- https://www.essentialvermeer.com/technique/technique_overview.html — the layered method: toned ground, laying-in, working-up wet-in-wet, scumbling and glazing; pointillé dots of lead white or lead-tin yellow on bread, pearls, brass and window edges.
- https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/object/The-Milkmaid--42dd0e658c2979aec8e144d2357c55c0 — The Milkmaid: the pouring milk as the only movement; bread on the table; pointillé on the crust and basket.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Milkmaid_(Vermeer) — the bread-and-milk dish; the foot warmer on the floor behind her; the bare wall with the nail.
- https://www.vermeerdelft.nl/en/blogs/did-johannes-vermeer-use-a-camera-obscura-or-not — the camera obscura question: soft focus and light dots as evidence for; no document, inventory or testimony that he owned one as evidence against.
- https://www.mauritshuis.nl/en/our-collection/artworks/92-view-of-delft — View of Delft seen from the southeast across the Kolk; the Schiedam and Rotterdam gates; the Nieuwe Kerk tower in morning sun; the gate clock a little past seven; figures waiting for a barge.
- https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/did-vermeers-girl-really-have-a-pearl-earring-180981638/ — the pearl may be glass, tin or only paint, and is too large to be real; the 2018 two-week study; no hook, two strokes of white.
- https://www.britannica.com/art/delftware — tin-glazed earthenware made at Delft from the early 17th century, imitating Chinese blue-and-white porcelain brought by the Dutch East India Company; cobalt blue under a clear glaze.
- https://museum.royaldelft.com/en/discover-the-collection/history/ — more than 30 potteries in and around Delft by the mid-17th century; De Porceleyne Fles the only one remaining.
- https://www.britannica.com/biography/Antonie-van-Leeuwenhoek — born in Delft in 1632, a draper; ground tiny single lenses of unmatched power; made hundreds of microscopes; the first to describe animalcules.
- https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rstb/article/370/1666/20140344/22476/The-unseen-world-reflections-on-Leeuwenhoek-1677 — 1674 water from the Berkelse Mere: green clouds, green streaks wound in spirals, and very many little animalcules moving.
- https://www.oudeennieuwekerkdelft.nl/en/new-church/tower/carillon/ — the Nieuwe Kerk's first carillon: 36 bells cast by François Hemony and hung in the tower in 1660.
- https://www.essentialvermeer.com/delft/delft_today/oude_and_nieuwe.html — the Oude Kerk (founded 1246) with its brick tower leaning about two meters, probably over a filled canal; the Nieuwe Kerk on the Markt, its tower completed in 1496.
