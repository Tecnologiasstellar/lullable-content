# Research sources — Leonardo's Notebooks by Candlelight

Verified 2026-09-14 by web search before writing. One line per source: URL — the fact(s) it supports.

- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci — born 15 April 1452 in or near the hill town of Vinci (hamlet of Anchiano); apprenticed to Verrocchio in Florence c. 1466 at about fourteen; master in the Guild of St Luke by 1472; moved to Milan in 1482 to the court of Ludovico Sforza.
- https://eclecticlight.co/2019/03/16/leonardo-the-apprentice-verrocchio-and-his-studio/ — Verrocchio's bottega as one of the best in Florence; training in drafting, metalwork, casting, plaster, carpentry as well as painting and sculpture.
- https://erenow.org/biographies/leonardo-da-vinci-the-flights-of-the-mind/42.php — Leonardo lived and worked in the Corte Vecchia in the 1490s, the old Visconti court on the south side of the cathedral square, superseded by the Sforza castle.
- https://www.duomomilano.it/en/about-us/the-candoglia-quarry/ — the Duomo's pinkish Candoglia marble came by water (Toce, Lake Maggiore, Ticino, Naviglio) to a quay in Milan; the cathedral was begun in 1386 and was still building.
- https://news.artnet.com/art-world/art-bites-leonardo-mirror-writing-2473530 — left-handed mirror writing, right to left, readable in a mirror; smudge-avoidance as the leading explanation; wrote the ordinary way for texts meant for others; "code" theory.
- https://arspictoria.com/leonardo-drawing-tools/ — silverpoint on prepared paper, red chalk, black chalk, pen and iron-gall ink from goose quills; paper from linen and hemp rags.
- http://faculty.goucher.edu/eng240/oak_gall_ink.htm — iron-gall ink: oak galls (tannin) plus iron sulfate (copperas) plus gum; the ink bites into the surface and darkens/browns with age.
- https://www.rct.uk/collection/912579/recto-studies-of-water-and-a-seated-old-man-verso-architectural-studies — Windsor sheet of water flowing past an obstacle; Leonardo's note comparing the motion of the water's surface to hair.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_on_the_Flight_of_Birds — c. 1505, 18 folios, 21 x 15 cm, Royal Library of Turin; observations of wing articulation, balance, and air resistance, then flying machines.
- https://artsandculture.google.com/story/ahead-of-his-time-leonardo-and-the-codex-leicester-the-british-library/DAVhNR_kmhU1ww ; https://www.odranoel.de/index.php?lang=eng&menu=codex&area=wasser&page=17 — Codex Leicester folio 2r: earthshine explained as sunlight reflected from Earth (he credited the oceans; clouds do most of it); folio 36r: sky is blue because lit moisture in the air is seen against the darkness beyond.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Leicester — 18 sheets / 72 pages on rag paper, c. 1506–1512; water, moon luminosity, fossils; in private hands since 1994 and exhibited city to city.
- https://www.aapg.org/news-and-media/explorer/the-geological-thinking-of-leonardo-da-vinci/ — shells found high in the hills lie in ordered layers, not tumbled as a flood would leave them; the land was once sea floor, buried in river mud and slowly lifted.
- https://www.fromoldbooks.org/Richter-NotebooksOfLeonardo/section-6/item-295.html — aerial perspective: distant things look bluer and paler through the mass of air; paint the farther things proportionately bluer and softer.
- https://www.fromoldbooks.org/Richter-NotebooksOfLeonardo/section-8/item-416.html — leaves inserted so that the sixth sits above the first (a spiral in fives); leaves turn their upper face to the sky for the night dew; branch and trunk thickness rules.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitruvian_Man — c. 1490, pen and brown ink over metalpoint, Gallerie dell'Accademia, Venice, rarely displayed; span of arms equals height, face one tenth, foot one seventh; after Vitruvius.
- https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/93/ — the Last Supper, 1495–1498, on the refectory wall of Santa Maria delle Grazie, Milan; painted in tempera on dry plaster rather than true fresco so he could work slowly; about 460 x 880 cm; thirteen figures.
- https://www.openculture.com/2025/09/leonardo-da-vincis-to-do-list-from-1490.html — the c. 1490 list: ask Benedetto Portinari how they go on ice in Flanders; a master of arithmetic to show how to square a triangle; a hydraulics master to explain repairing a lock, canal and mill in the Lombard manner; the measurement of the sun promised by Maestro Giovanni Francese; measure the Corte Vecchio; draw Milan.
- https://mymodernmet.com/leonardo-da-vinci-to-do-list/ — later lists remind him to buy chalk, charcoal and paper; his inventory of clothes and his liking for rose-colored garments.
- https://www.medievalists.net/2023/07/leonardo-da-vinci-wrote-these-great-fables/ ; https://news.artnet.com/art-world/try-10-leonardo-riddles-2049619 — the fable of the stone that rolled down from the flowers to the road and regretted it (Milan years, 1487–1494); short jokes about friars; riddles written as mock prophecies, likely for the Sforza court.
- https://www.nationalgeographic.com/premium/article/leonardo-davinci-notebooks-diaries-renaissance-man — roughly 5,000–7,000 surviving notebook pages, believed to be a fraction of what he wrote; pocket notebooks carried on his belt.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Atlanticus — 1,119 leaves in twelve bound volumes at the Biblioteca Ambrosiana, Milan; named for the atlas-size sheets.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Windsor ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Arundel — 606 sheets in the Royal Collection at Windsor; 283 leaves (c. 1480–1518) in the British Library, London.
- https://amboise-valdeloire.com/en/leonardo-da-vinci-at-the-chateau-du-clos-luce/ — 1516, aged 64, crossed the Alps on a mule with three paintings; the manor of Clos Lucé (pink brick and pale stone) given by Francis I, a short walk from the royal castle; the Amasse stream in the grounds, a tributary of the Loire; title of First Painter, Engineer and Architect to the King; the story of an underground passage to the castle.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divina_proportione — Luca Pacioli came to the Sforza court in 1496; Leonardo drew the polyhedra (shown hollow, as if of wood, hanging from ribbons); finished in Milan by 1498, printed 1509; the only book he illustrated.
- https://www.britannica.com/technology/mitre-gate — the mitre lock gate credited to Leonardo for the Milan canals: two leaves meeting in a V pointing upstream, held shut by the water's own pressure; still the design of the largest locks.
- https://www.rct.uk/collection/912363/cats-lions-and-a-dragon ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci,_A_Memory_of_His_Childhood — the late Windsor sheet of cats (c. 1517–18) drawn to study how the spine flexes; the Codex Atlanticus note recalling a kite (nibbio) coming to his cradle and touching his lips with its tail.
