# Research sources — The Glass Palace in Hyde Park

- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crystal_Palace — 1,848 ft long; 293,000 panes by Chance Brothers; about 4,000 tons of iron; 30 miles of guttering; Paxton's pink blotting-paper sketch of 11 June 1850; 72-ft barrel-vaulted transept enclosing full-size elms; the Duke of Wellington's "Sparrowhawks, Ma'am"; 80 men fitting 18,000 panes a week; Osler's 27-ft Crystal Fountain; Jennings toilets used 827,280 times; rebuilt at Sydenham, opened 10 June 1854.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Exhibition — opened 1 May 1851; 6,039,722 visits; ticket prices, one-shilling days Monday to Thursday after 22 May, 2s 6d Fridays, 5s Saturdays; Thomas Cook brought about 150,000 people; surplus of £186,000 funded the South Kensington museums.
- https://www.britannica.com/topic/Crystal-Palace-building-London — main body 1,848 ft by 408 ft; built in about nine months from foundations in September 1850 to the May 1851 opening.
- https://www.thegazette.co.uk/all-notices/content/100717 — 3,300 columns, 2,224 girders, 3,800 tons cast and 700 tons wrought iron, 900,000 sq ft of glass; designed by Paxton, erected by Fox, Henderson and Co.
- https://www.royalparks.org.uk/read-watch-listen/great-exhibition-1851-hyde-park — 14,000 exhibitors, 100,000 objects, six million people, May to October 1851; park trees ended up inside the building; moved pane by pane to south London in 1854; footprint still visible along South Carriage Drive.
- https://www.revolutionaryplayers.org.uk/glazing-the-crystal-palace/ — Chance Brothers of Smethwick; panes 49 in by 10 in, one-sixteenth inch thick, made by the blown-cylinder method; 293,655 panes shipped; the building's module scaled to the pane size.
- https://www.gracesguide.co.uk/Chance_Brothers_and_Co — Chance Brothers glassworks, Smethwick, supplier of the Crystal Palace glass.
- https://exhibitcitynews.com/the-biggest-id-project-in-history/ — the Paxton gutter: rain into channels at the bottom of each furrow, channels that served as rails for the glaziers' trolleys during construction; ridge-and-furrow roof from Paxton's glasshouses; hollow columns for drainage.
- https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260191352_Creating_the_right_internal_climate_for_the_Crystal_Palace — canvas shades over the roof against glare, wetted for evaporative cooling; louvres in the walls worked by wheel and cord; gaps in the floorboards for ventilation and sweeping.
- https://thegardenhistory.blog/2018/07/14/joseph-victoria-and-the-amazing-leaf/ — Paxton as head gardener at Chatsworth; Victoria regia (now V. amazonica) seed received 1849, first flowered in England November 1849; the purpose-built lily house with warm, gently moving water; the ribbed leaf as the idea behind the Crystal Palace.
- https://romantic-circles.org/gallery/image/miss-annie-paxton-who-drest-fairy-stood-upon-leaf-victoria-regina-chatsworth-1849 — Annie Paxton, aged nine, dressed as a fairy, standing on a lily leaf at Chatsworth in November 1849, drawn for the Illustrated London News; Douglas Jerrold among the spectators.
- https://eandt.theiet.org/2019/10/14/eccentric-engineer-taking-inspiration-nature-crystal-palace — Paxton's blotting-paper sketch during a Midland Railway board meeting, based on the Chatsworth lily house.
- https://www.crystalpalacefoundation.org.uk/history/history-of-the-crystal-palace-part-1 — the name "Crystal Palace" coined by Douglas Jerrold in Punch, 13 July 1850 ("a palace of very crystal"); construction timeline.
- https://shaunadoeshistory.co.uk/2022/07/13/oslers-crystal-fountain/ — Osler's Crystal Fountain: 27 ft high, four tons of crystal glass, at the centre of the transept, the meeting point of the exhibition.
- https://www.rct.uk/collection/2800080/the-great-exhibition-1851-glass-fountain-by-osler — the Osler glass fountain, F. and C. Osler of Birmingham.
- https://simonwenham.com/the-great-exhibition-of-1851/ — Schweppes paid £5,000 to run the refreshment rooms; over a million bottles of soft drinks and almost as many Bath buns sold; no alcohol served.
- https://www.alliancehousefoundation.org.uk/single-post/2017/07/01/Temperance-The-Great-Exhibition-and-the-Crystal-Palace — the temperance character of the exhibition; no alcoholic drinks in the refreshment courts; Thomas Cook's temperance excursions.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Cook_%26_Son — Paxton encouraged Cook to bring workers from Yorkshire and the Midlands; Cook's excursion trains and Exhibition Herald.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flush_toilets_at_the_Great_Exhibition — George Jennings's public flush toilets, one penny, 827,280 users, clean seat, towel and comb; origin of "spend a penny".
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission_for_the_Exhibition_of_1851 — the £186,000 surplus used from December 1851 to buy some 86–87 acres in South Kensington for museums and colleges (V&A, Natural History Museum, Science Museum, Royal Albert Hall, Imperial College).
- https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/the-great-exhibition-of-1851 — 100,000 exhibits; over half British and colonial, the eastern half of the floor shared among 34 foreign nations; the V&A's origin in the exhibition.
- https://editions.covecollective.org/content/vegetation-great-exhibition — transepts under a barrel vault and open-air courts added to save the elm trees; the central transept designed for elms to grow inside.
- https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/631573 — the Russian Court with malachite furniture and doors by Demidov of St Petersburg; Sèvres porcelain and Lyon silks in the French display; Pugin's Medieval Court (victorianweb.org/history/1851/20.html).
