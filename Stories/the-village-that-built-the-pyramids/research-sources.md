# Research sources — The Village That Built the Pyramids

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza — original height 146.6 m (481 ft); base 230.33 m per side; about 2.3 million blocks; built c. 2600 BC over roughly two decades; bedrock levelled flat to within about 21 mm; Tura limestone casing cut on a slope and smoothed; Aswan granite blocks of 25–80 tonnes in the King's Chamber and the chambers above; arsenical copper chisels, wooden mallets, ropes and stone tools; tallest built structure for nearly four thousand years.
https://www.britannica.com/place/Great-Pyramid-of-Giza — Great Pyramid of Khufu, original height, block count and roughly twenty-year construction around 2560 BC.
https://aeraweb.org/projects/lost-city/ — Heit el-Ghurab, the workers' town about 400 m south of the Sphinx and bounded by the Wall of the Crow; excavation begun 1988 by Mark Lehner's AERA; over 7 hectares; galleries, bakeries, commissaries, workshops, small houses and royal silos in a walled compound.
https://msu-anthropology.github.io/daea-fs16/sites/heit-el-ghurab/heit-el-ghurab.html — layout of the site: houses, streets, bakeries, royal administrative building, and the long galleries that housed rotating crews.
https://www.academia.edu/36358497/Bread_and_Bakeries_in_The_Heit_el_Ghurab_Site — the bell-shaped bread moulds (bedja) found in thousands; the flat trays used for a thinner bread; how the moulds were pre-heated in embers and stacked.
https://www.thearchaeologist.org/blog/the-giza-plateau-mapping-the-workers-village-and-bakery — dozens of bakeries capable of thousands of loaves a day; heavy bell-shaped bedja pots; bakeries paired with breweries.
https://www.livescience.com/28961-ancient-giza-pyramid-builders-camp-unearthed.html — Richard Redding's faunal work: about 25,000 sheep and goat, 8,000 cattle and 1,000 pig bones identified; roughly 1,800 kg of meat per day; cattle driven in from estates across Egypt; fish, beans and lentils in the diet.
https://lsa.umich.edu/lsa/news-events/all-news/search-news/the-diet-of-pyramid-builders.html — the workers were well fed; protein came half from fish, beans and lentils and half from sheep, goat and cattle.
https://www.harvardmagazine.com/2003/07/who-built-the-pyramids-html — Lehner and Hawass on a paid, rotating, conscripted workforce housed and fed by the state, not enslaved.
https://www.guardians.net/hawass/buildtomb.htm — the gang-and-phyle organisation; phyle names drawn from nautical words such as starboard and prow; crew names painted on blocks; the inscription recording workers paid in bread and beer.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khufu — the crew name read as "Friends of Khufu" in red paint in the relieving chambers above the King's Chamber.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid_of_Menkaure — the gang name usually rendered "Drunkards of Menkaure".
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diary_of_Merer — Merer the inspector (sehedj) and his team of about forty boatmen; year 26–27 of Khufu; limestone ferried from the Tura quarries to Giza; two or three round trips every ten days, about thirty blocks of 2–3 tonnes a trip; Akhet-Khufu, "Horizon of Khufu"; the harbour Ra-shi-Khufu; papyri excavated at Wadi al-Jarf in 2013 by Pierre Tallet and Gregory Marouard.
https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/ancient-egypt-shipping-mining-farming-economy-pyramids-180956619/ — the Wadi al-Jarf papyri as the oldest inscribed papyri known; the logbook's ordinary, administrative character.
https://archaeology.org/issues/july-august-2022/features/egypt-wadi-el-jarf-port-papyri/ — the account papyri recording rations of bread, beer and grain delivered to Merer's crew, and gifts of textiles.
https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.2202530119 — Nile waterscapes at Giza: the Khufu branch, the dredged harbour basins, and the annual flood of about seven metres used to bring cargo boats to the foot of the plateau.
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X23004789 — reconstruction of Khufu's harbour at Giza and its connecting channels.
https://phys.org/news/2014-04-ancient-egyptians-pyramid-stones-sand.html — the 2014 FOM / University of Amsterdam study in Physical Review Letters: damping the sand ahead of a sledge roughly halves the pulling force; too much water makes it worse again.
https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/simple-trick-may-have-helped-egyptians-build-pyramids-no-ancient-aliens-needed-180951317/ — the wall painting in the tomb of Djehutihotep showing a figure pouring water in front of a sledge hauling a colossal statue.
https://www.ancient-origins.net/artifacts-ancient-technology/pyramid-ramp-0013516 — the ramp question: straight, zigzag and spiral external ramps, none settled by evidence.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Houdin — the internal-ramp proposal of 2005 and the scepticism it has met.
https://africame.factsanddetails.com/article/entry-189.html — Petrie's suggestion that the base was levelled by cutting shallow trenches, flooding them and reducing the rock to the water line; the alternative sighting-rod method; the natural knoll left standing inside the base.
https://www.biblestudentarchives.com/documents/Nature_16Nov2000_Spence.pdf — Kate Spence in Nature: the simultaneous-transit method using Kochab and Mizar to find true north, and the narrow window in which that pair was accurate.
https://rpls.com/wp-content/uploads/wpforo/attachments/2627/14961-TrueNorth2013Feb-19-13-1.pdf — Glen Dash on the sides' deviation from true north of a few minutes of arc, and the solar-shadow alternative for finding a true east–west line.
https://apollo-magazine.com/great-pyramid-giza-egypt-casing-stone/ — the polished Tura casing, the slope-cut blocks and the extreme tightness of the joints.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_pyramid_construction_techniques — gypsum mortar spread thin in the joints, too weak to bond but slow-setting enough to act as a lubricant while a block was levered into its final position.
https://www.touregypt.net/featurestories/bread.htm — emmer wheat and barley; the saddle quern, and the miller's control over how coarse the flour came out.
