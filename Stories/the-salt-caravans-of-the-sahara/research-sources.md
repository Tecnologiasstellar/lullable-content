# Research sources — The Salt Caravans of the Sahara

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azalai — the azalai itself; twice-yearly departures from Timbuktu in November (cool season) and March; historical caravans of up to 10,000 camels on the Timbuktu route.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoudenni — Taoudenni is 664 km north of Timbuktu; salt from the bed of an ancient salt lake; pits 5 m x 5 m x 4 m deep; ~1.5 m of red clay overburden, then poor layers, then three good layers; slabs ~110 x 45 x 5 cm weighing ~30 kg; four or five slabs per camel; about three weeks by camel; route via the oasis of Araouane.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taghaza — Taghaza sat on an ancient salt pan (sabkha) 787 km north-northwest of Timbuktu; Ibn Battuta arrived in 1352 after 25 days from Sijilmasa; walls and roofs built of salt with camel hides for roofing; thick slabs, two of the great ones to a camel; food was dates from the north, millet from the south, camel meat; production shifted to Taoudenni after 1586.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Araouane — Araouane lies 243 km north of Timbuktu on the caravan route to Taoudenni; former entrepôt of the trans-Saharan trade; its wells; guides navigate by sand ripples and dune form by day and by the stars at night.
https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1342/the-salt-trade-of-ancient-west-africa/ — salt literally worth its weight in gold in parts of West Africa; salt used as currency in small pieces; salt for preserving food; Ibn Battuta on houses and mosques of salt blocks roofed with camel skins; Timbuktu as the middleman market.
https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1344/the-camel-caravans-of-the-ancient-sahara/ — caravan day of roughly 48 km; dawn start, rest through the midday heat, move again in the late afternoon and after dark; the khabir who finds the way by the stars at night and by the scent and touch of the sand; goods south (salt, cloth, dates, copper) and north (gold, grain, hides, kola).
https://www.bradshawfoundation.com/africa/tuareg_salt_caravans/index.php — caravan pace leisurely at first to take advantage of grazing, then marching hard; ~50 km a day; camp routine of unloading, fodder bundles as windbreaks, tea and boiled millet; fine salt grades for people and coarse grades for livestock; barter of three volumes of dates for two of millet.
https://www.nationalgeographic.com/pages/article/in-sahara-salt-hauling-camel-trains-struggle-on — the Taoudenni run of roughly 800 km; a single tracker leading by wind patterns on the sand, the stars, and the changing color of the sand; salt cut from an ancient seabed; blocks cut into smaller pieces and sold on through West Africa.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dromedary — hump stores up to 36 kg of fat; two toes on flat leathery pads about 19 cm across; drinks up to 114 L in one sitting at 10–20 L a minute; double rows of eyelashes; nostrils closed voluntarily; walking pace about 4 km/h.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4270234/ — the camel's body temperature drifts by several degrees over the day, storing heat rather than sweating it off, which conserves water.
https://seaworld.org/animals/facts/mammals/dromedary-camel/ — fat stored in the hump rather than water; rapid rehydration; tolerance of large water loss relative to body weight.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djinguereber_Mosque — Djinguereber built 1327 under Mansa Musa; designed by the Granada-born architect Abu Ishaq al-Sahili; mud-brick construction, three inner courts, twenty-five rows of pillars, prayer space for about 2,000; replastered by hand after the rains.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbuktu — Timbuktu's position where the desert meets the Niger bend; salt arriving by camel and leaving by river; the trading city as the meeting point of caravan and boat.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankor%C3%A9_Madrasah — Sankoré founded in the 14th–15th century; teaching in scholars' own houses; subjects including law, astronomy, mathematics and medicine; the three mosques of Djinguereber, Sankoré and Sidi Yahya.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbuktu_Manuscripts — tens of thousands of manuscripts, dating from the late 13th century onward; subjects including astronomy, mathematics, jurisprudence and medicine; written in Arabic and in African languages in Ajami script; kept loose in leather wrappers and chests.
https://hmml.org/stories/series-migration-to-timbuktu-from-a-land-far-away/ — European paper reaching Timbuktu across the Sahara; bales carried from Mediterranean ports through the oases to the Sudanic cities.
https://www.academia.edu/29546063/Imaging_endangered_manuscripts_materials_of_Timbuktu_manuscripts — Italian and southern French paper in the Timbuktu manuscripts; the "three crescents" watermark, the Venetian style later imitated by French mills.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran_boards — wooden writing tablets (lawḥ) of mahogany or baobab used by West African students, written on in ink, washed and reused; still in use today.
https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-treasures-of-timbuktu-138566090/ — about 150–180 Qur'an schools in Timbuktu in the mid-16th century; 123 writing boards counted in one of them.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabara,_Mali — Kabara is the river port of Timbuktu, 8 km to the south, linked to an arm of the Niger by a canal; salt carried down to the boats; usable at high water.
https://maisonnana1807.com/en/blogs/mintealogy/tuareg-tea-tradition — the three glasses of Saharan tea from the same leaves, the first strong, the second sweeter, the third light; poured from a height so the tea froths.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fennec_fox — smallest fox; ears up to about 15 cm, richly supplied with blood vessels close to the skin, radiating body heat; nocturnal, sheltering in a burrow by day; Saharan nights falling close to freezing.
https://open.oregonstate.education/middleearlymodernages/chapter/ibn-battuta-travels/ — Ibn Battuta's 1352 departure from Sijilmasa, the twenty-five days to Taghaza, the village of salt-block houses and mosque roofed with camel skins, the imported dates and millet, salt used as a medium of exchange.
https://www.britannica.com/animal/camel — camel kneeling on callused pads, load-carrying, dromedary size and gait.
