# Research sources — The Monk and the Pea Garden

- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregor_Mendel — born 20 July 1822 at Heinzendorf (Hynčice), Silesia; Augustinian friar at St Thomas's Abbey, Brno; University of Vienna 1851–1853, physics under Christian Doppler; pea experiments 1856–1863 with some 28,000 plants; seven traits; lectures 8 February and 8 March 1865; 1866 paper; rediscovery in 1900 by de Vries, Correns and Tschermak; founding member of the Austrian Meteorological Society 1865; bred Cyprian and Carniolan bees.
- https://www.gutenberg.org/files/40854/40854-h/40854-h.htm — Mendel's own 1866 text: 34 varieties bought from seed merchants, two-year trial, 22 kept without exception; emasculation by opening the unopened bud, removing the keel and lifting out each stamen with forceps, then placing pollen on the stigma; pot plants kept in a greenhouse during flowering as a control against insects; the pea beetle and only a few cases of stray pollen among more than 10,000 plants examined; F2 counts 5,474 round to 1,850 wrinkled (2.96:1) and 6,022 yellow to 2,001 green (3.01:1); pods 882 inflated to 299 constricted; unripe pod colour 428 green to 152 yellow; flowers 651 axial to 207 terminal; stems 787 tall to 277 short; average 2.98:1; the words dominant and recessive; plants grown in garden beds, a few in pots, tied to stakes.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experiments_on_Plant_Hybridization — paper read to the Natural Science Society of Brünn on 8 February and 8 March 1865; published 1866 in the Verhandlungen des naturforschenden Vereines in Brünn, vol. 4, pp. 3–47; the 9:3:3:1 two-trait ratio; rediscovery in the early twentieth century.
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5586364/ — the two 1865 lectures; a contemporary newspaper report that the first lecture was well received with lively audience participation; after the second, Professor von Niessl offered supporting remarks from microscopic observation.
- https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4299709/ — the 1865 presentation as the founding moment of genetics; 8 March 1865.
- https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/gregor-mendel-genetics — nearly 30,000 pea plants bred between 1856 and 1863 in the monastery garden; results presented 1865 to the local society; dominant and recessive patterns.
- https://courses.lumenlearning.com/ivytech-wmopen-nmbiology/chapter/the-father-of-genetics/ — experiments on Pisum sativum 1856–1863 at the Abbey of St Thomas, Brno; why peas: self-fertilising, easily crossed by hand, clear either-or traits; true-breeding lines established first.
- https://www.ck12.org/flexi/life-science/pea-plants/what-genetic-characteristics-did-mendel-study/ — the seven trait pairs: seed shape (round/wrinkled), seed colour (yellow/green), flower colour (purple/white), pod shape (inflated/constricted), pod colour (green/yellow), flower position (axial/terminal), stem length (tall/short).
- https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/genetics-and-genetic-engineering-biographies/gregor-johann-mendel — meteorological observations from 1857; recognised as the authority on weather in Moravia; wrote on transmitting weather data to Vienna and getting forecasts to farmers; apiary as a research site for controlled bee crossing; fuchsia breeding in the monastery garden and a marketable "Mendel fuchsia"; sent to Vienna in 1851; taught natural history and physics at the Brünn Realschule.
- https://www.sci.muni.cz/en/current-news/gregor-johann-mendel-meteorologist — measurements from at least 1857 at the monastery; air temperature, precipitation, air pressure, ozone, phenology, sunspot counts and groundwater level in the monastery grounds; records sent to the central institute in Vienna; readings at 7, 14 and 21 h in his later years.
- https://www.dnaftb.org/3/bio.html — physics under Doppler in Vienna; designed beehives and worked on breeding bees for honey yield; founding member of the Austrian meteorological society; tracked sunspots and their possible link to weather and the aurora; better known in his lifetime as a weather forecaster than for heredity.
- https://www.magiscenter.com/blog/gregor-mendel-priest-teacher-abbot-and-avid-beekeeper — taught physics and natural history at the Brünn Modern School (Realschule) 1854–1868; never obtained the full teaching certificate; co-founder of the Brünn beekeeping society; fruit-tree grafts and remnants of the apiary still in the monastery garden; the "Mendel fuchsia".
- https://www.sci.muni.cz/en/current-news/mendel-a-passionate-beekeeper — brick bee house built 1871 behind the abbey; around 50 colonies at his most active; member of the Moravian bee society from 1870; lectures printed in the society's magazine; experiments with Italian, Egyptian and Carniolan (Krainian) bees; Cyprian bee noted 1877.
- https://academic.oup.com/ae/article/69/2/40/7219101 — Mendel's beekeeping and its relation to his pea work; crossing bee races to observe traits.
- https://fuchsietum.com/blog/files/mendel-and-his-fuchsias.php — Mendel grew and crossed fuchsias in the monastery garden and in the abbey greenhouse built for him by the abbot; a cultivar Fuchsia 'Mendel' was sold commercially; he is shown examining a fuchsia in a group photograph of the friars taken after 1860 and in a second portrait.
- https://www.em.muni.cz/en/news/15912-mendel-s-greenhouse-restored-in-museum-garden — the original abbey greenhouse dates from 1854; only its foundations survived; the 2022 reconstruction copies the original layout and orientation.
- https://english.radio.cz/peas-will-grow-there-once-again-new-mendel-greenhouse-opens-brno-8766989 — the greenhouse built in 1854 by Abbot Cyrill Napp for Mendel; in the grounds of the Augustinian Abbey of St Thomas, Brno.
- https://www.gotobrno.cz/en/place/mendels-greenhouse/ — only the foundations of Mendel's original greenhouse survived; the rebuilt greenhouse stands on them.
- https://gregormendel200.org/gregor-mendel-2/viennese-influences/ — Vienna 1851–1853: physics and mathematics under Doppler and Ettingshausen; plant anatomy, physiology and the microscope under Franz Unger; Doppler's influence on his experimental method.
- https://www.britannica.com/biography/Gregor-Mendel — return to Brünn in 1853; appointed in 1854 to teach physics and natural history at the Realschule, where he stayed until elected abbot fourteen years later.
- https://www.genome.gov/25520238/online-education-kit-1900-rediscovery-of-mendels-work — 1900 rediscovery by Hugo de Vries, Carl Correns and Erich von Tschermak, each working independently on plant hybrids and each citing Mendel's 1866 paper.
- https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/rediscovery-mendels-laws-heredity — the three botanists reached the same conclusions independently and then found Mendel's paper; the eight-year study of 22 true-breeding varieties.
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9335201/ — Mendel's father Anton was a farmer and orchardist who kept bees; the boy helped with grafting, pruning and the hives; Abbot Napp accepted him into the abbey in 1843 and supported his career.
- https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3897355 — the Sheep Breeders' Society of Moravia; Abbot Napp a leading member; by 1837 Napp framed the question "what is inherited and how?" which later motivated Mendel's pea work.
