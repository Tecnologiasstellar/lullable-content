# Research sources — The Fossil Hunter of Lyme Regis

- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Anning — born 1799 in Lyme Regis; father Richard a cabinetmaker who sold fossils to visitors; local names snake-stones, devil's fingers, verteberries; Joseph found the 4-ft ichthyosaur skull in 1811, Mary the rest (17 ft) months later; sold to Henry Hoste Henley for £23, shown at Bullock's museum on Piccadilly, later the British Museum; plesiosaur December 1823 with 35 neck vertebrae, Conybeare 1824, Cuvier's doubt resolved; Dimorphodon December 1828, Buckland 1829 credited her, Owen renamed it; coprolites (letter to Buckland 1824, published 1829); Anning's Fossil Depot on Broad Street 1826 with glass storefront and ichthyosaur noted by local papers; belemnite ink sacs 1826 and Philpot's fossil ink; hand-copied papers, cement-on-frame preparation, dissection of fish and cuttlefish; Featherstonhaugh buying for New York 1827; 1829 London visit as guest of the Murchisons; Blue Lias alternating limestone/shale, winter rain exposing fossils.
- https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/mary-anning-unsung-hero.html — Natural History Museum biography: ichthyosaur skull found by Joseph 1811, skeleton by Mary 1812 (~5 m); plesiosaur 1823; Dimorphodon 1828; coprolites; her role informing Buckland, De la Beche and Conybeare.
- https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9626254/ — the 1811–12 ichthyosaur (holotype of Temnodontosaurus platyodon) collected by Joseph and Mary Anning; Everard Home gave the first scientific description.
- https://www.geolsoc.org.uk/the-library/online-exhibitions/mary-anning-and-the-geological-society/reconstruction-of-life/plesiosaurus-dolichodeirus/ — plesiosaur found 10 December 1823, about 3 m by 2 m; bought by the Duke of Buckingham for 150 guineas; shown at the Geological Society on 20 February 1824; too large for the stairs so displayed in the hallway; now at the Natural History Museum.
- https://paleonerdish.wordpress.com/2024/03/29/on-the-bicentenary-of-mary-annings-plesiosaurus/ — Conybeare's 1824 paper; Cuvier's initial doubt and April 1824 acceptance; Constant Prévost's June 1824 visit to Lyme, buying a plesiosaur for Paris.
- https://www.geolsoc.org.uk/the-library/online-exhibitions/mary-anning-and-the-geological-society/pterosaurs-coprolites-and-sepia/fossil-sepia/ — belemnite ink sacs (fossil sepia) identified by Anning, noted by Buckland 1829, confirmed by Agassiz 1834; Elizabeth Philpot's ichthyosaur-skull drawing in fossil ink c. 1833–34 sent to Mary Buckland.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Philpot — born 1779; came to Lyme with her sisters; fossil-fish collection; reconstituted belemnite ink with water; Agassiz's 1834 visit, 34 species shown, "utter certainty", fish named Eugnathus philpotae.
- https://wessexcoastgeology.soton.ac.uk/Lyme-Regis-to-Charmouth.htm — Blue Lias ~32 m thick at Lyme, Hettangian to lower Sinemurian; alternating shales, marls and limestones; laid down in fairly warm water of moderate depth over a muddy floor; named stone bands (Table Ledge, Broad Ledge) quarried for cement; Coroniceras and Arietites ammonites, Gryphaea, belemnites, Pentacrinites; storms and rain exposing fossils; Church Cliffs and Black Ven.
- https://ukfossils.co.uk/2003/02/27/lyme-regis/ — Blue Lias ~199–183 million years old; pyritised ammonites; ichthyosaur bones; Black Ven and the Shales-with-Beef; best collecting after winter storms at low tide.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Lias — Blue Lias formation, limestone and clay layers in the cliffs around Lyme Regis and Charmouth, Lower Jurassic, ammonites and marine reptiles.
- https://www.lyellcollection.org/doi/abs/10.1144/GSL.JGS.1923.079.01-04.05 — Shales-with-Beef: Lias above Table Ledge, seams of fibrous calcite called "beef" by the Geological Survey officers; descends to the beach at Charmouth below Black Ven.
- https://wessexcoastgeology.soton.ac.uk/liasfos.htm — Blue Lias ammonite zones: smooth or weakly ribbed Psiloceras in the lowest zone, strongly ribbed and keeled, larger Arietites higher up.
- https://www.bgs.ac.uk/discovering-geology/fossils-and-geological-time/belemnites/ — belemnites: squid-like swimmers with an internal calcite guard ("thunderbolts").
- https://www.bgs.ac.uk/discovering-geology/fossils-and-geological-time/crinoids/ — crinoids (sea lilies): stalk of stacked ossicles, crown of feathery arms, filter feeding.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentacrinites — star-shaped stem ossicles; Pentacrinites bed of the Lower Lias at Lyme Regis; attachment to floating driftwood.
- https://natmus.humboldt.edu/exhibits/fossil-focus-exhibits/what-ammonite — ammonite as extinct cephalopod: coiled chambered shell, septa, siphuncle for buoyancy.
- https://www.forbes.com/sites/davidbressan/2016/08/30/the-origin-of-geological-terms-ammonites/ — Pliny's "horns of Ammon", the ram-horned god, origin of the name.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duria_Antiquior — De la Beche's 1830 watercolour/lithograph "A more ancient Dorset", first pictorial scene of prehistoric life from fossil evidence, creatures from Anning's finds, prints by Scharf.
- https://www.bristolmuseums.org.uk/blog/the-bristol-institution-and-mary-anning-a-pioneering-palaeontologist/ — Bristol Institution: Conybeare and De la Beche bought its first fossil from her and donated it in 1823; many of her fossils sold to Bristol.
- https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2017/07/she-sells-seashells-and-mary-anning-metafolklore-with-a-twist/ — Library of Congress: no evidence links the tongue-twister or Terry Sullivan's 1908 song to Mary Anning; the twister predates the song (in print by 1871).
- https://www.lymeregismuseum.co.uk/collection/mary-anning/ — Lyme Regis Museum stands on the site of her birthplace; "first complete ichthyosaur 1810–11"; plesiosaur 1823; pterosaur 1828.
- https://www.strangescience.net/anning.htm — her dissection of a cuttlefish: ink bag matching the fossil, and a second small gizzard-like pouch with horny pieces; comparing fossils with living relatives.
