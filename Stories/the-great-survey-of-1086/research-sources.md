# Research sources — The Great Survey of 1086

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domesday_Book — survey planned 1085 and carried out 1086; Great Domesday written by a single scribe; Little Domesday covers Essex, Norfolk and Suffolk and is the work of at least six scribes; 13,418 places recorded; originally called Liber de Wintonia / Book of Winchester; custody at Winchester, then Westminster, then the Public Record Office in 1859, then The National Archives at Kew; 1986 rebinding of the two books into five volumes; still cited in court cases over manorial and common rights; online since 2006.
https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/domesday-book/ — more than 13,000 places mentioned; Great Domesday written by one scribe and checked by a second; Little Domesday the work of at least six scribes and probably written first; oldest government record held at The National Archives.
https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/modern-europe/british-and-irish-history/domesday-book — survey commissioned at the Christmas court at Gloucester in 1085; England divided into roughly seven circuits; commissioners sat in the county courts and received the reports of local juries; each manor recorded with holder in 1066 and in 1086, assessment, peasantry, ploughs and ploughlands, churches, mills, woodland; Great Domesday the edited version, Little Domesday the unedited East Anglian draft.
https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/domesday-survey — kingdom divided into seven circuits of three to six counties each; work begun as early as January 1086 and complete by the court at Salisbury on 1 August 1086; 888 leaves; editing at Winchester, possibly by one scribe.
https://academic.oup.com/ehr/article/135/576/1085/6044073 — commissioners sent to every shire, juries assembled in each hundred, sworn testimony on who held what land now and at the start of William's reign; jurors drawn in equal numbers of Englishmen and Frenchmen.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ely_Inquiry — the Ely Inquiry (Inquisitio Eliensis) as a satellite text of the 1086 survey; sworn reports of sheriffs, barons and their Frenchmen, the whole hundred, the priest, the reeve and six villagers of each vill; answers given for three time points (King Edward's time, when King William gave it, and now).
https://wiki.vikingsonline.org.uk/Domesday_Book — the list of questions from the Inquisitio Eliensis: the name of the place; who held it before 1066 and now; how many hides; how many ploughs in lordship and of the men; how many villagers, cottagers and slaves, free men and sokemen; how much woodland, meadow and pasture; how many mills and fishponds; how much added or taken away; what the total value was and is; all threefold, before 1066, when King William gave it, and now.
https://www.worldhistory.org/Domesday_Book/ — juries made up 50-50 of Englishmen and Normans to ensure fairness in doubtful claims; counties grouped into circuits; survey process completed within a year; name in common use from the 12th century, from "doom" or the Day of Judgement; book now held by The National Archives.
https://manors.org.uk/guide/domesday-book — the standard shape of an entry: manor and location, hidage as tax assessment, ploughland capacity, population by category, resources (woodland, meadow, pasture, mills, fisheries, salthouses), and three valuations — in King Edward's time, when the lord received it, and in 1086.
https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/decoding-domesday/domesday-past-and-present/3714B0751D9E6293D8A0DFF7A8D831EC — the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle's account of the "deep speech" at Gloucester at Christmas 1085 about the land and how it was peopled; the chronicler's remark that not one hide, not one ox, cow or pig escaped notice (paraphrased in the script, not quoted).
https://www.csmonitor.com/1986/0423/zdome.html — about 2 million words; 888 leaves; parchment from the skins of between 500 and 1,000 sheep; written in black and red ink; 1986 rebinding in tawed pigskin over quarter-cut English oak boards, the two volumes reorganised into five.
https://facts.uk/facts-about-the-domesday-book/ — parchment from sheepskins soaked in lime, scraped of residual hair, stretched on wooden racks and dried; quill pens from goose feathers with slanted tips; headings and important details in red ink, general text in black; the books now at The National Archives at Kew.
https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-018-0215-0 — condition study of the Domesday Books: iron gall ink for the text; red pigments ranging from vermilion in Great Domesday to red lead in Little Domesday; conservation rebinding programme begun 1984 ahead of the 900th anniversary, rebinding the two original volumes as five to reduce stress from the over-tight 1952 binding.
https://www.domesdaybook.net/domesday-book/great-domesday — Great Domesday almost entirely the work of a single scribe, in highly abbreviated medieval Latin; counties beginning on a new folio with the county name as a red running head; place-names capitalised and lined in red; Little Domesday never reduced to the standard format.
http://www.windmillworld.com/watermills/domesday.htm — 6,026 mills recorded in Domesday, 4,844 of them valued; mills assessed in pounds, shillings and pence, average value about 12 shillings; some mills rendering eels instead of money (e.g. a mill at Tempsford rendering 120 eels; one at Horningsea 1,000).
https://opendomesday.org/place/TL1653/tempsford/ — worked example of a village entry: hundred and county, recorded households (villagers, smallholders, slaves), ploughlands and lord's and men's plough teams, one mill valued at 10 shillings, annual value to the lord in 1086, when acquired, and in 1066; lords named for 1066 and 1086.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liber_Exoniensis — Exon Domesday, the only surviving circuit return, covering Cornwall, Devon, Dorset, Somerset and Wiltshire; written by many hands; 532 parchment leaves; contains livestock tallies, lists and summaries omitted from Great Domesday.
https://www.exeter-cathedral.org.uk/history-heritage/cathedral-treasures/exon-domesday/ — Exon Domesday is a unique surviving draft of the 1086 survey and has been kept at Exeter Cathedral (MS 3500) since at least the 14th century.
https://www.domesdaybook.net/domesday-book/data-terminology/manors/livestock — livestock recorded mainly in Essex, Norfolk and Suffolk and sometimes in the south-western counties; sheep by far the most numerous animal, followed by pigs, cattle, goats and cobs (small working horses).
https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/domesday-book/ — mills, fisheries, salt works and vineyards among the resources recorded; the salt industry the most fully documented industry in Domesday.
https://www.domesdaybook.net/domesday-book/domesday-inquest — the inquest procedure: commissioners assembling juries in each hundred and taking sworn answers to a fixed questionnaire.
